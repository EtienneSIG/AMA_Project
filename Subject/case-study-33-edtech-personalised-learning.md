# Case Study 33 — AI-Driven Personalised Learning Platform for a European EdTech Group

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Industry** | Education Technology |
| **Headquarters** | Netherlands |
| **Operating Region** | Netherlands, Belgium, Germany, Poland, and Romania |
| **Regulatory Context** | GDPR · EU AI Act · Sector-specific EU Directives |

---

## Business Challenge

A Dutch EdTech company delivering digital learning solutions to **4.1 million learners** across K-12 and vocational education in five European countries faces:

- Learner outcomes varying by up to **40%** between high- and low-performing schools using identical content
- Teacher time spent on administrative assessment consuming **35% of working hours**
- Content localisation for national curricula requiring **8–12 months per market entry**
- **GDPR children's data rules (Article 8)** constraining personalisation for learners under 16
- **EU AI Act** high-risk classification for AI systems influencing educational outcomes requiring human oversight

---

## Transformation Objective

Build a **privacy-preserving personalised learning platform** that adapts content to individual learner needs, reduces administrative burden on teachers, and accelerates curriculum localisation within GDPR and EU AI Act frameworks.

## Expected Outcomes

- Learner outcome gap between high- and low-performing schools reduced by **26%**
- Teacher administrative time reduced by **45%**
- Content localisation time reduced from **12 months to 6 weeks**
- Full **GDPR Article 8** compliance maintained

---

## AI Infusion Point

A **privacy-preserving learner model** adapts content difficulty and pacing without storing individual identifiable data.

A **curriculum localisation AI** translates and adapts learning content to national standards.

An **automated assessment AI** grades structured assignments and provides formative feedback to learners.
